How we source components for V2M One in the US

A sample article showing how V2M could explain component sourcing, prototype testing and hardware decisions to clients, partners and service teams.

By V2M Team

This is a sample article for testing the page layout. It does not describe a confirmed V2M sourcing process.

A hardware product starts with a set of requirements, but every requirement eventually becomes a physical part. Sensors, batteries, connectors and enclosures all affect how a device works and how easily it can be built and maintained.

This example outlines the questions an engineering team might ask when choosing those parts. The final article can replace these examples with real decisions, photographs and test results.

Starting with requirements #

Before comparing individual components, a team needs to define what the complete device should do. That includes its operating environment, expected battery life and the way people will handle it during everyday work.

  • What measurements does the device need to capture?
  • How often will it be used during a working day?
  • Which conditions should the enclosure withstand?
  • How will a technician charge, inspect or replace parts?

Designing for everyday use #

A component can perform well on a laboratory bench and still be inconvenient in a workshop. A connector, for example, also needs to be easy to reach and straightforward to connect. These practical details belong in the requirements from the beginning.

Comparing components #

Price is only one part of a comparison. Documentation, availability and mechanical compatibility can also affect whether a component is suitable for a prototype or a production design.

A useful comparison explains why a part fits the application, not just which specification is highest.

Example criteria — not actual V2M specifications
Component What to compare Example check
Sensor Measurement range and repeatability Compare readings under controlled conditions
Battery Capacity, dimensions and charging requirements Measure runtime with a representative workload
Connector Retention, accessibility and expected wear Check repeated connection and disconnection

Testing a prototype #

A prototype helps turn assumptions into observations. A simple test record can identify the hardware revision, describe the conditions and keep results together for later comparison.

  1. Record the component and hardware revision.
  2. Define the test conditions and expected behaviour.
  3. Run the same procedure for each candidate.
  4. Document results and questions for the next revision.

The following illustrative record uses hardware_revision to identify the prototype: 

{
  "hardware_revision": "prototype-a",
  "component": "sample-sensor",
  "test": "repeatability",
  "status": "pending"
}

A finished article can include measured results here, explain any trade-offs and connect the engineering decision to what it means for the people using V2M One.
