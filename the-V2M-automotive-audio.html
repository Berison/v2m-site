The V2M automotive audio archive

By Aleksanr Iovlev, Igor Kordunsky, Andrey Maltsev

In this rather short article we want to expand on the data we base our acoustic diagnostics research on. This is going to be a follow-up to our previous article on the evolution of the V2M software .

How we record audio

Since 2021, when the idea of acoustic research was put into action and the company was founded, we’ve been working on a reliable way of collecting audio data from vehicles, both in normal operation and with faulty components (you can read more about our hardware design journey in our earlier article ).

By 2023, we had a fully functional and relatively reliable device (actually, a couple of them first, but we never stopped building them), which we used to start recording and storing audio continuously. For this, we took advantage of having full access to the V2M founder’s main venture at the time: a 15-year-old car repair shop in central Moscow. With full-time access to literally any equipment and expertise needed to diagnose and disassemble cars, we made the most of what we had. For about 16 months, we recorded every single ride of the vehicles representing the main platforms we were interested in at the time (Tesla, Volvo, and Lexus) and kept a daily log of component conditions.

This operation produced around 37 000 hours of vehicle sounds, which became the foundation of our current collection of hums, squeaks, whistles, knocks, clunks, clicks and other peculiar sounds. Some of these sounds are normal and some are not, and reliably distinguishing between them using an automated system became the focus of our research.

As of October 2026, we store around 60 000 hours of expertly labeled data collected on roads around the globe, including in our main cities of operation: Moscow, Dubai and Boston. The data covers multiple combinations of road surfaces and conditions (asphalt, concrete, cracks, pots, bumps, grooves etc) in quite diverse weather conditions, whether it’s dry, snow, rain or mud, temperatures varying from below freezing to unbearably hot for a human.

All of this adds distortions on how mechanisms sound in both normal and fault states, ultimately contributing to the robustness of our recognition algorithms.

Built for reuse

We record audio from cars operating normally and from cars with confirmed malfunctions, and we also make recordings of the same car again after the faulty component has been replaced and the malfunction fixed, to use these recordings for either training or testing our models. We keep the original raw audio data, along with any available (anonymized) metadata: GPS, date and time, speed, the vehicle’s model, make and mileage, the vehicle’s condition and any noted fault or repair, which devices captured the audio, any confirmed recording problems, such as a failed microphone or incorrectly installed sensors. Audio can be recut and relabeled for new tasks, such as pretraining on unlabeled audio, detecting unusual sounds, classifying sound types, locating events and studying the effect of recording conditions.

What cars we recorded

Across several periods, we logged faults of varying severity in hub bearing, ball joints, splash guards, engine mount, and differential bearings along with belt or brake squeals, turbo whistle etc., and recorded the same cars after the faulty components were repaired. These vehicles represent the most popular car platforms, including:

  • Small cars: Toyota Yaris, Suzuki Swift, Renault Symbol.
  • Crossovers: Honda CR-V, Volkswagen Tiguan.
  • Full-size pickups: Ram 1500, Chevrolet Silverado, Toyota Tundra.
  • SUVs and off-roaders: Nissan Patrol, Cadillac Escalade, Land Rover Defender.
  • Electric vehicles: Tesla Model 3 and Model X.
  • Luxury and performance models: BMW 7 Series, Mercedes-Benz S-Class, Porsche Cayenne, Rolls-Royce Wraith, Lamborghini Urus.

All this data has allowed us to achieve 98% accuracy on detecting the vehicle’s fault state, which exceeds the hearing abilities of an average human technician, and to shorten the diagnostics time to minutes.
